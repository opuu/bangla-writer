# Genres

Naturalness is genre-specific. A sentence that is perfect in a text message is wrong in a court notice, and a line that works in a poem would be a failure in a lab report. This reference gives, for each common genre, what natural means there, the usual form of address, and the failure it most often falls into.

None of this overrides the grammar rules, which hold everywhere. What changes is vocabulary, sentence length, how much is left unsaid, and how much English is welcome.

## Everyday messages and speech

Texts, chat, voice notes, quick notes at home or work.

Natural here means short, elliptical, and full of particles. Subjects drop freely (`কাল আসছি`), compound verbs carry most of the meaning (`দেখে নিস`, `বলে দিয়ো`), and English words that people actually say stay in (`ফোন`, `স্কুল`, `ব্যাগ`, `টাইম`). Address matches the real relationship — `তুমি` or `তুই` with family and friends, `আপনি` with elders and strangers.

Most common failure: writing full, well-formed sentences with every subject and object present. `আমি কালকে তোমার বাড়িতে আসব` is grammatical and sounds like a form letter next to `কাল তোর বাড়ি আসছি`.

## Letters and personal correspondence

Letters, emails to relatives, condolences, congratulations, invitations, thanks.

Bengali letters have their own furniture: an opening address (`প্রিয় মা`, `শ্রদ্ধেয় স্যার`, `প্রিয় বন্ধু`), a health enquiry near the top (`কেমন আছ?`, `আশা করি ভালো আছ`), and a closing (`ইতি`, `তোমার স্নেহের`, `বিনীত`). Condolence and congratulation letters lean on established formulas; using them is correct, not lazy.

Most common failure: importing an English business-letter skeleton — subject line, three body paragraphs, "I hope this email finds you well" rendered literally as `আশা করি এই ইমেইল আপনাকে ভালো অবস্থায় পাবে`, which means nothing in Bengali.

## Literary prose and fiction

Stories, novels, memoir, personal essays.

Naturalness here means fidelity to a voice, not conformity to standard prose. Fragments, unusual word order, repetition, dialect, and long unpunctuated runs may be the point. Dialogue should sound spoken — regional forms, `যামু`, `করস`, `বুঝলা`, and half-finished sentences belong in a character's mouth even when they never belong in the narration.

Keep narration and dialogue in different registers unless the writer is deliberately merging them. Do not standardize a character's speech into the narrator's Bengali.

Most common failure: smoothing. Merging deliberate fragments, replacing an odd but chosen word with a common one, and adding connectives that flatten the rhythm.

## Poetry and song

Only edit at the writer's instruction, and never silently.

Meter, rhyme, line breaks, and archaic or সাধু diction can all be deliberate — সাধু forms in verse are not গুরুচণ্ডালী দোষ. If asked to translate verse, say what you traded away (sound, meter, or literal sense); do not quietly choose one and present it as the poem.

## Journalism and reportage

News, features, interviews.

News Bengali is compact and attributive. Attribution goes at the end (`বলে জানিয়েছেন জেলা প্রশাসক`), the lede carries the fact rather than a scene, numbers and place names are exact, and unverified claims stay marked (`অভিযোগ`, `জানা গেছে`, `সূত্র জানায়`). Third person and honorific verbs for named adults are the norm in both regions.

Most common failure: editorializing while translating. An adjective added for flow (`ভয়াবহ`, `নজিরবিহীন`) becomes a claim the reporter did not make.

## Editorial, opinion, and column

Naturalness here is argument, not decoration. State the position, give the reason, keep the hedges the writer chose. Rhetorical questions and direct address are allowed if the columnist uses them, but a stack of `সময়ের সাথে সাথে`, `উল্লেখ্য যে`, `সামগ্রিকভাবে বলা যায়` is the sound of an argument that is not being made.

Most common failure: inflating a modest claim into a sweeping one, or ending every paragraph with a summarizing sentence.

## Essays, exams, and academic writing

School essays (রচনা), university assignments, research prose.

Bengali academic writing tolerates more nominalization and more formal vocabulary than English does, and its passive-like impersonal frames (`দেখা যায়`, `বলা যেতে পারে`, `গবেষণায় প্রমাণিত হয়েছে`) are correct, not translationese. Terminology stays fixed across a whole paper — one Bengali or English rendering per concept.

Do not casualize an academic register in the name of naturalness. Do keep hedges exact: `সম্ভবত`, `অনেকাংশে`, `কিছু ক্ষেত্রে` carry the argument's honesty.

Most common failure: the school-essay opening — `বর্তমান প্রেক্ষাপটে ... একটি গুরুত্বপূর্ণ ভূমিকা পালন করে` — used as though it were content.

## Official, civic, and legal text

Government notices, circulars, applications (দরখাস্ত), contracts, rulings, forms.

This genre has fixed formulas that must be kept: `এতদ্বারা জানানো হইতেছে` or its modern `এতদ্বারা জানানো যাচ্ছে`, `বিনীত নিবেদন এই যে`, `স্মারক নং`, `সদয় অবগতির জন্য`, `যথাবিহিত সম্মান প্রদর্শনপূর্বক`. Some agencies still write সাধু throughout; that is their style, not an error to fix.

Precision beats flow. Preserve section numbers, dates, deadlines, monetary amounts, and named authorities exactly, and keep modality exact: `করিতে হইবে`/`করতে হবে` (must) is not `করা উচিত` (should). Use the jurisdiction's official term rather than a translated guess — Bangladesh and West Bengal name offices, courts, and school stages differently.

Most common failure: modernizing a formula that carries legal weight, or softening an obligation into a suggestion.

## Business and workplace

Emails, memos, meeting notes, proposals, HR notices.

`আপনি` by default. Be direct and courteous without bureaucratic padding: `সময় হলে দেখে জানাবেন` beats `আপনার সুবিধাজনক সময়ে পর্যালোচনা সম্পন্ন করিয়া মতামত প্রদান করিবেন`. English business vocabulary is normal in both regions (`মিটিং`, `ডেডলাইন`, `রিপোর্ট`, `আপডেট`) and forcing Bengali equivalents reads as stiff.

Most common failure: `অনুগ্রহ করে` on every line, and nominalized verbs — `পর্যালোচনা সম্পন্ন করা` for `দেখা`.

## Education and instructional text

Textbooks, lesson notes, tutorials, how-to guides, children's material.

Order the steps as the reader will do them, use imperatives at a consistent politeness level, and introduce a term once with its English form in brackets if the learner will meet it again. For young readers, keep sentences short, concrete, and free of Sanskritized vocabulary; for older students, define rather than avoid.

Most common failure: explaining in adult register and calling it simplified, or switching between `তুমি` and `আপনি` between exercises.

## Health, safety, and public information

Clinic instructions, medicine labels, public-health messages, disaster warnings.

Clarity is a safety property. State what to do, when, and what happens if it is not done. Keep dosage, frequency, and duration exact and unambiguous. Keep modality exact — "may cause" is `হতে পারে`, not `হবে`. Avoid fear-based phrasing unless the source uses it. Prefer the word the patient will recognize (`জ্বর`, `রক্তচাপ`, `ওষুধ`) over the clinical coinage.

Most common failure: rounding a hedge into a certainty, or translating a dosage frame loosely.

## Speeches and spoken delivery

Anything meant to be heard: speeches, scripts, podcasts, announcements, voiceover.

Write for the ear. Short clauses, one idea per breath, no nested relative clauses, no parentheses. Repetition is a tool here, not a flaw. Read it aloud; if you run out of breath or lose the subject, restructure.

Most common failure: written-Bengali syntax with long modifier chains that a speaker cannot land.

## Subtitles and captions

Reading speed and line length rule everything. Compress without changing meaning, break lines at clause boundaries, and keep one speaker per subtitle. Spoken dialect in the audio usually becomes readable standard Bengali on screen unless the dialect is the point.

Most common failure: full-fidelity translation that nobody can read in time.

## Marketing and promotional

Ads, landing pages, campaign copy, social posts.

Energy is allowed; invention is not. Take the specifics from the brief and keep the claim exactly as strong as the source made it. Match the brand's chosen `আপনি` or `তুমি` and keep it. A concrete benefit outperforms `যুগান্তকারী`, `অভূতপূর্ব`, or `নতুন দিগন্ত উন্মোচন`.

Most common failure: manufacturing features or evidence to fill a sentence that sounded empty.

## Social media and community writing

Posts, comments, captions, community notices.

Register varies enormously by platform and circle. Match the surrounding voice: heavy code-switching and Roman-script Bengali may be normal in one community and wrong in another. Do not convert Roman-script Bengali to Bengali script unless asked; do not add hashtags or emoji the writer did not use.

## Religious and ceremonial

Sermons, prayers, festival greetings, ritual announcements, obituary notices.

Established formulas and vocabulary matter more than freshness, and they differ by community — Islamic, Hindu, Christian, and Buddhist Bengali each have their own set. Reproduce quoted scripture exactly and do not paraphrase it. When unsure of a formula, keep the source's wording rather than inventing a smoother one.
