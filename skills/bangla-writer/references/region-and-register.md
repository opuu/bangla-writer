# Region and register

Use this reference when location, audience, or social relationship affects the wording. These are tendencies, not borders: individual writers and publications vary, and many forms are current in both regions.

## Regional choice

If the user specifies a house style or supplies representative text, follow it. Otherwise:

- For Bangladesh, prefer contemporary standard usage familiar in Bangladeshi public and professional writing.
- For West Bengal, prefer contemporary standard usage familiar in Indian Bengali public and professional writing. Call this "West Bengal Bengali" or "Indian Bengali, especially West Bengal" rather than "West Bengali", which names a person, not a variety.
- For a mixed or unspecified audience, choose widely understood vocabulary and avoid conspicuously local words when a neutral alternative is easy.

Coherence is the goal, not purity. A text should not swing between varieties, but a form that is ordinary in both regions needs no replacement, and a writer who uses one local word inside otherwise neutral prose is not making an error.

### Lexical markers

| Meaning | Often in Bangladesh | Often in West Bengal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| water | পানি | জল |
| breakfast/light meal | নাশতা | জলখাবার |
| bathing | গোসল | স্নান |
| salt | লবণ | নুন |
| invitation | দাওয়াত | নিমন্ত্রণ |
| maternal aunt | খালা | মাসি |
| paternal aunt | ফুফু | পিসি |
| address, older man | ভাই / ভাইয়া | দাদা |
| address, older woman | আপা | দিদি |

`আমন্ত্রণ` and other neutral words sit outside this table and are safe in both regions. Note that `দাদা` in Bangladesh specifically denotes a paternal grandfather (or elder brother in Hindu families), whereas in West Bengal `দাদা` is the general word for elder brother and the standard respectful address for any adult man (where grandfather is `দাদু` or `ঠাকুরদা`). Do not mechanically replace every item in a source: `জল`, `পানি`, and their counterparts may be deliberate, idiomatic, or genre-specific. Maintain the established voice unless localization is requested.

### Non-lexical markers

Vocabulary is the obvious signal, but these carry locale just as strongly in written text and are easier to get wrong:

- Spelling: ই-কার in `জরুরি`, `দাবি`, `শ্রেণি` reads Bangladeshi; ঈ-কার in `জরুরী`, `দাবী`, `শ্রেণী` reads West Bengal. See [orthography-and-typography.md](orthography-and-typography.md).
- Plural suffix: `-গুলো` is the everyday Bangladeshi form, `-গুলি` more common and more formal in West Bengal.
- Third person: colloquial West Bengal writing uses `ও`, `ওরা`, `ওকে` freely where Bangladeshi writing more often uses `সে`, `তারা`, `তাকে`.
- Colloquial negation: `যাই নাই`, `করি নাই` are Bangladeshi spoken forms. Standard written Bengali in both regions uses `যাইনি`, `করিনি`.

Administrative and educational terms can differ more sharply across countries. When precision matters, use the jurisdiction's official term instead of guessing from a word-pair list.

## Forms of address

Choose a relationship and keep the pronoun, possessive, and verb form aligned:

- `আপনি` for respectful, professional, unfamiliar, or formally addressed readers
- `তুমি` for familiar equals, friendly brands, and many instructional contexts when invited by the voice
- `তুই` only for a clearly intimate, affectionate, confrontational, or character-specific voice

Examples of agreement:

- `আপনি কী জানতে চান?`
- `তুমি কী জানতে চাও?`
- `তুই কী জানতে চাস?`

Avoid switching between `আপনি` and `তুমি` merely to vary wording. A brand may use an impersonal construction to avoid choosing, but do not make every sentence passive.

## Register

Register is the formality level and the distance between writer and reader. It is not the same as genre — a letter can be intimate or official, a report can be plain or ceremonial. For what each kind of text needs, see [genres.md](genres.md); this section covers only how formality itself behaves in Bengali.

### The levels

- **Intimate** — family and close friends. `তুই` or `তুমি`, dropped subjects, particles (`রে`, `না`, `তো`), regional forms welcome.
- **Conversational** — familiar equals, friendly brands. `তুমি` or `আপনি`, short clauses, everyday loanwords, compound verbs carrying most of the meaning.
- **Professional** — colleagues, customers, strangers addressed with respect. `আপনি`, direct and courteous, ordinary verbs over nominalizations.
- **Formal and institutional** — notices, applications, ceremonies. Complete forms, established formulas, stable terminology.
- **Literary** — whatever the writer's voice demands, including deliberate violations of everything above.

### Moving between levels

Formality in Bengali comes from vocabulary, sentence completeness, and honorific forms — not from archaic inflection. Raising the register means `দেখুন` for `দেখ`, `নথি` for `কাগজ`, a full subject where speech would drop it. It does not mean `করিয়াছেন` for `করেছেন`: সাধু forms inside চলিত prose are গুরুচণ্ডালী দোষ, not extra politeness. Use সাধু only when the user asks, the genre requires it (some official formulas, verse, scripture), and then use it throughout.

Lowering the register means dropping subjects, shortening clauses, and letting in the words people say — not sprinkling slang. See "Do not manufacture naturalness" in [naturalness-patterns.md](naturalness-patterns.md).

### Two things stay constant at every level

`অনুগ্রহ করে` is useful once and mechanical by the third repetition. And whatever level you choose, the pronoun, possessive, and verb must agree with it from the first sentence to the last.
