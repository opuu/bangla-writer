# Grammar and syntax

Use this reference when editing sentence-level grammar or when a translation follows the source language too closely. These rules hold in every genre, from a text message to a court notice. The examples describe common standard-colloquial patterns; dialect, genre, emphasis, and house style can justify other forms.

## Negation

Choose the negative construction from the predicate rather than substituting one negative word mechanically.

- `নেই` marks absence, non-existence, or lack of possession: `ঘরে কেউ নেই`, `আমার সময় নেই`.
- `নয়` and honorific `নন` negate identity, classification, or a nominal attribute: `এটা আমার বই নয়`, `তিনি ডাক্তার নন`.
- `না` negates a verb, imperative, infinitive, or subordinate action: `এখন যেও না`, `না জেনে কিছু বোলো না`.
- `-নি` negates the past and the perfect. It is a suffix, not a separate word: `আমি বুঝিনি`.

Word order changes with construction and focus, so `না` is not always placed after a finite verb. Preserve an idiomatic negative expression when it already works.

### Absence, identity, and possession are three separate repairs

Correcting a wrong negator must not change who or what the sentence is about.

Malformed:

> চিঠিটা টেবিলে না।

Corrected — absence:

> চিঠিটা টেবিলে নেই।

Malformed:

> এটা কোনো সমস্যা নেই।

This one has two possible readings, and they take different repairs. If the source means "there is no problem in it", the subject takes the locative or genitive:

> এতে কোনো সমস্যা নেই। / এটার কোনো সমস্যা নেই।

If the source means "this is not a problem", keep the nominative and negate the identity:

> এটা কোনো সমস্যা নয়।

Pick the reading the source supports. If the source genuinely does not decide it, preserve the ambiguity or ask, rather than choosing silently.

### Past and perfect negation with `-নি`

`-নি` attaches to the present-tense stem and carries the past meaning by itself: `করিনি`, `যাইনি`, `দেখিনি`, `তুমি পাঠাওনি`, `তিনি বলেননি`.

- `আমি করেছি না` and `আমি করেছিলাম না` are not Bengali. The perfect has no `না` negation at all.
- `সে আসে না` means he does not come, habitually. `সে আসেনি` means he did not come or has not come. Translating an English simple past with `আসে না` is a meaning error, not a style choice.
- Simple past with `না` is possible but marked: `আমি গেলাম না` reads as a decision not to go at that moment, not as neutral past negation.
- `নাই` for `-নি` is common in Bangladeshi colloquial speech and dialogue (`যাই নাই`). Keep it only where that voice is intended, not in standard written prose.

## Tense and aspect

English tense does not map one-to-one onto Bengali. Choose the aspect the Bengali sentence needs, then check it against the source's actual time reference.

- The Bengali present perfect covers much of the English simple past in ordinary speech and writing. `চিঠিটা পাঠিয়েছ?` translates both "Did you send the letter?" and "Have you sent the letter?" `পাঠিয়েছিলে` adds remoteness the English rarely has.
- The simple present carries habit and general truth: `আমি ওই রাস্তা দিয়ে যাই না`, not `যাচ্ছি না`, for "I don't go that way."
- The past habitual `-তাম` translates "used to" and "would": `আগে রোজ হেঁটে যেতাম`.
- Reserve the continuous for action genuinely in progress. English uses the progressive for scheduled and stative meanings where Bengali does not: "We are leaving next month" is `আগামী মাসে চলে যাচ্ছি` or `আগামী মাসে যাব`, and "I am liking it" is simply `ভালো লাগছে`.
- The past perfect `করেছিলাম` marks a remoter past or an event before another past event. Do not use it merely because the English says "had" in a narrative frame the Bengali does not need.

Before:

> তুমি কি গতকাল চিঠিটি পাঠিয়েছিলে? আমি এখন ওই পথে যাচ্ছি না।

After:

> কাল চিঠিটা পাঠিয়েছ তো? ওই পথে আমি যাই না।

## Compound verbs (যৌগিক ক্রিয়া)

Bengali attaches a vector verb to a non-finite `-এ` base to carry completion, preparation, direction, or stance. Bare verbs are grammatical but often read flat, and this is one of the clearest markers of writing conceived in Bengali.

- `ফেলা` — completion, suddenness, irreversibility: `খেয়ে ফেলেছি`, `লিখে ফেলো`
- `রাখা` — doing something in advance or keeping a result: `রান্না করে রেখেছি`, `কথাটা মনে রেখো`
- `নেওয়া` — action for oneself, checking, deliberation: `দেখে নাও`, `বুঝে নিন`, `খেয়ে নে`
- `দেওয়া` — action directed at or for someone else: `পাঠিয়ে দিন`, `বলে দিয়ো`
- `যাওয়া` — unintended process or change of state: `ভুলে গেছি`, `চিঠিটা হারিয়ে গেছে`
- `ওঠা` / `বসা` — abruptness, or an action regretted: `কেঁদে উঠল`, `রাজি হয়ে বসেছি`

The vector changes meaning, so choose it from what is actually true. `মুছুন` is a neutral instruction; `মুছে ফেলুন` says the erasure is final, which matters when it is not. `বলো` is neutral; `বলে দাও` says the telling is on someone else's behalf. Do not add a vector to every verb — stacked vectors and long chains sound as artificial as bare ones.

## Experiencers, needs, and possession

Bengali often expresses bodily states, involuntary experiences, needs, liking, or possession with genitive/dative-like subjects:

- `আমার শীত করছে।`
- `আপনার কি সাহায্য লাগবে?`
- `রিমার গানটা ভালো লেগেছে।`
- `আমার একটি প্রশ্ন আছে।`

Do not force every mental-state verb into this pattern. `আমি গানটা পছন্দ করি`, `আমি বিষয়টি বুঝি`, and many other nominative constructions are natural. Choose the construction that fits the predicate and voice.

## Passive and impersonal frames

Bengali has a passive, but it is narrower than the English one. A `দ্বারা` agent phrase is rare outside legal and scientific registers, and copying one is the fastest way to sound translated. See [naturalness-patterns.md](naturalness-patterns.md) for the transformation patterns.

- Obligation: `কাল আবেদন করতে হবে` / `আবেদনকারীকে কাল আবেদন করতে হবে`
- Agentless report: `সিদ্ধান্ত নেওয়া হয়েছে`, `সভা স্থগিত করা হয়েছে`
- Capability or availability: `এখান থেকে টিকিট পাওয়া যাবে`

Keep an explicit passive with `দ্বারা` or `কর্তৃক` when the genre requires naming the agent formally, as in a legal notice or a citation.

## Reflexive reference

Use `নিজে`, `নিজের`, or `নিজেকে` when the referent is the clause subject and the non-reflexive form could point to someone else.

- Potentially ambiguous: `শিক্ষক তার বইটা নিয়ে এসেছেন।`
- Explicitly reflexive: `শিক্ষক নিজের বইটা নিয়ে এসেছেন।`

Do not replace `তার` or `তাঁর` when it deliberately refers to a different person. If the source is ambiguous and the task is only rewriting, preserve or flag the ambiguity instead of inventing a referent.

## Honorific agreement

Keep person, honorific level, possessive/object form, and verb inflection coherent.

- `তিনি আমাকে বললেন।`
- `তাঁকে বিষয়টি জানানো হয়েছে।`
- `আপনি একবার দেখে নিন।`
- `তুমি একবার দেখে নাও।`
- `তুই একবার দেখে নে।`

In Bengali, the chandrabindu (`ঁ`) on third-person pronouns is strictly an honorific marker, not a nasal vowel. Keep the agreement bidirectional:

- Respected / formal (`তিনি`, `উনি`, `যিনি`): `তাঁর`, `তাঁকে`, `তাঁদের`, `তাঁরা`, `ওঁর`, `ওঁকে`, `ওঁদের`, `ওঁরা`, `যাঁর`, `যাঁকে`, `যাঁদের`.
- Familiar / ordinary (`সে`, `ও`, `যে`): `তার`, `তাকে`, `তাদের`, `তারা`, `ওর`, `ওকে`, `ওদের`, `ওরা`, `যার`, `যাকে`, `যাদের`.

Never drop `ঁ` in respectful reference (e.g., `তিনি ... তার` is a mismatch), never add `ঁ` to non-honorific `সে/তার`, and never pair `তিনি` with a non-honorific verb such as `বলল`.

## Affixes, particles, and postpositions

Distinguish attached inflections and particles from separate postpositions:

- **Case inflections (বিভক্তি)** and enclitic particles attach directly to the preceding Bengali word:
  - `বাড়িতে`, not `বাড়ি তে`
  - `মায়ের`, not `মা এর`
  - `আমাকে`, not `আমা কে`
  - `আজকেই`, not `আজ কে ই`
  - `আমিও`, not `আমি ও`
- **Postpositions (অনুসর্গ)** are separate lexical words and take a space:
  - `আপনার জন্য`, not `আপনারজন্য`
  - `শহর থেকে`, not `শহরথেকে`
  - `তার মতো`, not `তারমতো`
  - `টেবিলের উপরে`, not `টেবিলেরউপরে`
  - `নিয়ম ছাড়া`, not `নিয়মছাড়া`

With Latin-script words, preserve readability and the user's convention. A hyphen often makes the boundary clear: `server-এ`, `API-টির`, `ChatGPT-কে`. Never alter code identifiers, paths, URLs, or quoted strings merely to attach a Bengali suffix. This rule is the same one applied to product strings in [product-and-technical-language.md](product-and-technical-language.md).

## Classifiers and numbers

Use classifiers when Bengali grammar or the intended register calls for a counted individual:

- `তিনটি চিঠি` / conversational `তিনটা চিঠি`
- `চারজন অতিথি`
- `এক জোড়া জুতা`

Classifiers are not mandatory after every number. Units, durations, ages, percentages, dates, labels, and some institutional styles commonly use a bare number plus noun or unit:

- `দুই বছর`, `তিন কিলোমিটার`, `চার শতাংশ`
- `৫ জানুয়ারি`, `ধারা ৩`, `বাস ১২`

Choose `-টি` or `-টা` by register and noun, not as a universal formal/informal substitution.

## Plural marking

### Animacy decides the suffix

- `-রা` and `-দের` mark humans and animals: `ছাত্ররা`, `শিক্ষার্থীদের`, `পাখিরা`, `অতিথিদের`.
- `-গুলো` and `-গুলি` mark things and generalized collectives: `বইগুলো`, `গাছগুলি`, `কথাগুলো`.
- `চিঠিরা` and `সমস্যারা` are not Bengali outside deliberate personification, which poetry does use: `গাছেরা দাঁড়িয়ে আছে`.
- `ছাত্রগুলো` or `লোকগুলো` exists in dismissive or very colloquial speech. It is wrong in formal or respectful text, where `ছাত্ররা` / `ছাত্রদের` is the only correct choice.
- `-গুলি` reads more formal and is more common in West Bengal; `-গুলো` is the everyday form in Bangladesh. Keep one of them throughout a document.

### Do not double-mark

Avoid redundant plural marking when one quantifier already supplies the meaning:

- Usually tighter: `সব বই`, not `সব বইগুলো`
- Usually tighter: `সকল শিক্ষার্থীকে`, not `সকল শিক্ষার্থীদেরকে`

But quantified classifier forms can be natural when the set itself is in focus: `সবগুলো বই`, `এই কয়েকজন শিক্ষার্থী`. Preserve such distinctions instead of deleting every plural suffix after a quantifier.

## Non-finite clause chaining

Related actions often flow naturally through non-finite verbs instead of repeated `এবং` or `তারপর`:

Before:

> আমি বাজারে গেলাম এবং তারপর চাল কিনলাম এবং তারপর বাড়ি ফিরলাম।

After:

> বাজারে গিয়ে চাল কিনে বাড়ি ফিরলাম।

Do not build a long chain whose actor, sequence, or scope becomes unclear. Split steps in safety-critical instructions or when separate actions need separate emphasis.

## Conditionals

For a simple condition, `-লে` or emphatic `-লেই` is often lighter than a full `যদি … তাহলে` frame:

- `বৃষ্টি হলে আমরা যাব না।`
- `কাজ শেষ হলেই খবর দেবেন।`

Keep `যদি … তাহলে` when it clarifies a long condition, contrasts alternatives, or carries deliberate emphasis. Do not replace it automatically.

## Relative and participial clauses

A participial modifier can simplify a rigid source-language relative clause:

- `যে চিঠিটি হারিয়ে গিয়েছিল, সেটি পাওয়া গেছে` → `হারিয়ে যাওয়া চিঠিটি পাওয়া গেছে`.

Keep a full relative-correlative construction when it prevents ambiguity or carries contrast. When used, maintain clear pairing such as `যা … তা`, `যেমন … তেমন`, `যেখানে … সেখানে`, or `যেহেতু … সেহেতু`.

## Topic, focus, and information structure

Bengali commonly puts the known or contrastive element early and the new information late. Fronting moves a constituent:

- Neutral: `বইটা আলমারিতে আছে।`
- Location as topic: `আলমারিতে বইটা আছে।`
- Contrast fronted: `এই বইটা আজ লাগবে না, ওইটা লাগবে।`

The emphatic clitic `-ই` marks focus without moving anything: `আলমারিতেই বইটা আছে।` — the almirah is where it is, and nowhere else. Fronting and `-ই` are separate tools and can combine.

Do not front every object as a style trick, and do not scatter `-ই` for rhythm. Both change emphasis; retain the source's intended emphasis.

## Duration and purpose

Use forms such as `ধরে` or an idiomatic duration construction for elapsed, continuing time: `দুই ঘণ্টা ধরে অপেক্ষা করছি`. Use `জন্য` for purpose, beneficiary, or an allocated span: `দুই দিনের জন্য ঘরটি বুক করেছি`. Context may permit more than one form, especially with future duration.
